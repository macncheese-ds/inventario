## 4. ESTIMATE MATERIAL CONSUMPTION AND TIME UNTIL STOCK DEPLETION

Add a function that estimates approximately how long each material or applicable inventory item will last, based on its current available stock and the frequency and quantities of historical stock decreases.

The purpose is to answer: “At the current historical consumption rate, approximately how many days will this material remain available, and when might it run out?”

This is an **estimate**, not a guaranteed depletion date.

### Inspect the existing data before designing the calculation

- Determine how materials, spare parts, stock balances, and inventory movements are represented.
- Identify which movement types represent actual consumption.
- Distinguish consumption from receipts, replenishments, transfers, corrections, write-offs, returns, canceled transactions, and reversals according to the application's verified business rules.
- Do not count every negative stock movement as consumption without verifying its meaning.
- Determine whether stock is managed globally, by warehouse, by location, or through another existing scope. Keep the stock balance and consumption history within the same scope.
- Determine what “available stock” means in the current application. If reservations or committed quantities exist, follow the verified availability rules rather than inventing a new definition.
- Check timestamp precision, timezone conventions, quantity precision, and units of measure.
- Identify whether historical data is complete enough to support the estimate.
- If consumption cannot be reliably distinguished using existing records, explain the limitation and propose the smallest appropriate change through the database approval process.
- Do not reconstruct missing history from the current stock balance or present invented movement dates as real data.

### Calculation requirements

Use a transparent, explainable calculation rather than an unnecessarily complex forecasting system.

Propose the observation period, minimum history requirements, and treatment of incomplete history for my approval before implementation. Do not silently choose business-sensitive defaults.

The baseline approach should be:

- Calculate total qualifying consumption within the agreed observation period.
- Divide that consumption by the number of calendar days in the valid observation period to obtain average daily consumption.
- Include days with no consumption. Do not calculate the average using only days on which a withdrawal occurred.
- For newly created items or incomplete history, use only a defensible observation period supported by the data and disclose its limitations.
- Estimate days remaining by dividing current available stock by average daily consumption.
- Derive the approximate depletion date from the estimate using the application's established date and timezone conventions.
- Account for both withdrawal frequency and withdrawal quantities through the historical consumption rate. Do not estimate depletion from transaction counts alone.
- Keep units consistent between current stock and historical consumption. Do not combine incompatible units or introduce unverified conversions.
- Define how returns, reversals, and corrected consumption affect the calculation, based on verified workflows.
- Document rounding rules. Do not round intermediate values in ways that materially distort the result.
- Recalculate or invalidate cached results when relevant stock balances or qualifying historical movements change, including edits, cancellations, and backdated entries.
- Do not include future replenishments or pending purchases in the baseline estimate unless a separate, explicitly approved requirement calls for them.

Do not introduce machine learning, external forecasting services, complex statistical models, or new dependencies for this feature.
